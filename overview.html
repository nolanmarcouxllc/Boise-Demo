The ten

What each one watches for

Every example below comes from the sample day in the demonstration. Real numbers, made-up branch.

1

Two trips become one

Finds orders heading the same direction so one truck can take them all.

Three orders near Northampton. One truck instead of two. 74 fewer miles.

2

Same stop twice

Notices when two trucks are sent to the same customer on the same day.

Two trucks to the same yard, 95 minutes apart.

3

Missing details

Checks an order has everything routing needs before anyone plans it.

Four orders had no delivery window or no unload equipment.

4

Trucks on the same road

Shows where two routes drive the same stretch of highway.

Two routes share 31 miles in each direction.

5

Who takes what

Lays out a few ways to split the work between trucks, and what each way costs.

Three plans: fewest miles, best on-time, fullest trucks.

6

Something changed late

When an order changes after planning, shows what it breaks.

Six units added while the truck was loading. The load order has to change.

7

Our truck or hire one

Says which is better, and why the cheaper one is not always right.

Keep one route. Hire out another — our truck can't unload it.

8

What really happened

Compares the plan to the actual day, so next week's plan is closer.

One stop ran 48 minutes long — and has run long 4 of the last 6 times.

9

One list of problems

Everything that needs attention in one place, with a name against each one.

15 things today. 5 urgent. 1 with nobody's name on it.

10

The morning summary

One short page for the manager. Click any line to see what it is based on.

Three decisions waiting before the trucks leave the yard.

The system catches it. The person decides it.

Not one of these forty sends a truck, changes an order, or calls a customer on its own. They notice, they explain, and they wait for someone to say yes.

What this is. A working concept built on made-up orders, customers and routes, to show what a connected view could look like. Nothing is connected to Boise Cascade's real systems, and none of the numbers describe the real Westfield branch. The next step is a working session with operations and IT to find out which of these are worth building — and which are wrong.
